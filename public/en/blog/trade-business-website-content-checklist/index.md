# What content does a trade business website need?
Canonical URL: https://www.vibeperform.com/en/blog/trade-business-website-content-checklist/
German alternate: https://www.vibeperform.com/de/blog/handwerker-website-inhalte-checkliste/
Summary: Services, real work examples, service area and contact details: a practical checklist for preparing the content of your trade business website.

Topic: Website planning
Published: 2026-09-30
Author: Marlon Dietrich
Reading time: 4 min read

A trade business website should help customers prepare a concrete decision: Does this business handle my type of project, work in my area and provide a clear way to enquire? Start with the questions your team repeatedly answers on the phone. Those answers become a useful page plan.

This checklist is a planning aid. The pages you need depend on your services and customers. A small, clearly explained website can be more helpful than many pages with almost identical text.

## 1. Explain services in terms customers recognise

List your main services and describe each one: Who is it for? What work do you take on? What information do you need for an enquiry? Where does your scope end?

A dedicated page makes sense when a service answers distinct questions. Closely related work can share a page. Use your customers’ vocabulary and explain technical terms where they are needed.

**Illustrative example, not a client reference:** A joinery business offers fitted wardrobes. Rather than saying only “bespoke interiors”, its page explains wardrobes for alcoves and sloping ceilings. It describes consultation, material selection and installation, and asks for photos and approximate dimensions with an enquiry. A firm price or date follows only after the business has reviewed the request.

## 2. Gather the essential content

| Content | What to prepare | What to check before publication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | Main services, customer groups and actual region | Can someone outside the business immediately understand your offer? |
| Service pages | Work, process, requirements and boundaries | Do the details reflect what you offer today? |
| Business and contacts | Who works here, relevant experience and who receives enquiries | Are roles and substantiated qualifications accurate? |
| Work examples | Your own photos, starting point, work completed and a short explanation | Are image rights and necessary permissions settled? |
| Contact | Current phone number, email, enquiry route and any visitor arrangements | Do test enquiries reach the responsible person? |
| Area and availability | Actual service area and accurate hours | Are visits by appointment and telephone availability clear? |

Work examples do not require public customer names or exact private addresses. Explain what made the task interesting and what was actually done. Generated images can serve as illustrations, but should not pretend to show real projects.

Required legal details and privacy information also belong in the plan. Have them reviewed for your actual business and website functions by an appropriate specialist; this content checklist does not replace that review.

## 3. Finish each page with a relevant next step

A visitor to a service page needs a clear contact route. State which information makes an enquiry useful: the work requested, location and existing documents, for example. Ask only for information needed at this stage.

Test the journey on a smartphone: Can you use the telephone link? Complete the form? Understand what happens after submission? Promise response times or available dates only when your business can support the commitment.

## 4. Make content easy to find and read

Google recommends clear, current content and descriptive page titles and links. Its [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) explains these foundations. In practice, give each page a distinct title, use clear subheadings and link related services where doing so helps the reader.

Give relevant images useful alternative text. Avoid artificially repeating locations and service keywords. Text length follows the customer questions you need to answer; Google does not specify an ideal minimum word count for rankings.

## 5. Agree ownership and review

Preparation requires current service information, usable images and someone who consolidates feedback from the business. Start with a list of existing content, missing content, responsible person and next decision.

Before publication, the business confirms facts, service scope and image use. Presentation, links and contact routes are then checked. A clear handover to [ongoing website support](/en/website-support/) helps with later changes.

When contact details or services change, review the business profile too. [Aligning your Google Business Profile and website](/en/blog/google-business-profile-website-consistency/) explains how the two fit together.
