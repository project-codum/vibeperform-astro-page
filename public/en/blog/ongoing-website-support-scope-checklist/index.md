# What belongs in ongoing website support?
Canonical URL: https://www.vibeperform.com/en/blog/ongoing-website-support-scope-checklist/
German alternate: https://www.vibeperform.com/de/blog/website-betreuung-umfang-checkliste/
Summary: Update content, check contact routes and plan improvements: a checklist for agreeing the tasks, responsibilities and approvals in website support.

Topic: Keeping your website current
Published: 2026-09-30
Author: Marlon Dietrich
Reading time: 4 min read

A website changes with the business. Services are added, contacts change and useful examples of work emerge. Ongoing support organises those changes: What needs updating, who provides the information and who reviews the result?

“Website support” alone says little about scope. Content updates, technical maintenance and development are different tasks. State explicitly which ones are agreed for your website.

## 1. Separate three areas of work

**Content updates** include new contact details, revised service descriptions, selected project photos or changed visitor hours. The business supplies and confirms the facts. The person supporting the website prepares them for the agreed pages.

**Technical tasks** depend on the system. A system with plugins and a database has different maintenance needs from a statically generated website. Establish who handles hosting, the domain, updates, backups and possible incidents. A general support label does not automatically include these services.

**Development** means prioritising specific improvements: a clearer service page, an easier-to-find contact form or more suitable image sizes, for example. A major redesign or additional features need a separate agreement.

## 2. Use a checklist to agree scope

| Question | What to record in writing |
| --- | --- |
| Which website is covered? | System, pages, languages and affected areas |
| Which changes are included? | Change types, agreed scope and how extra work is handled |
| Who supplies material? | Person responsible for facts, images and consolidated feedback |
| Who publishes? | Preview, factual approval and publication responsibility |
| Which technical work is covered? | Specific tasks and responsibilities for hosting, domain and maintenance |
| How are problems reported? | Contact route and response times that have actually been agreed |
| How is work recorded? | A short record of completed changes and outstanding items |
| What happens at handover? | Access, documents and the agreed website handover |

Access should have permissions appropriate to the task. Website, domain and analytics access must not be known only to one external person at handover. Establish actual account ownership before work starts.

## 3. Plan updates around real needs

A useful support plan follows changes in the business. New contact details or incorrect opening hours have a different priority from an additional project report. Record the trigger, responsible person and desired timing for each task.

There is no blanket obligation to publish a post every week. [Google recommends helpful, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content). Use real customer questions and current information as reasons to update; do not change a date simply to make unchanged text appear newer.

**Illustrative example, not a client reference:** An electrical business starts offering an additional service. The owner confirms its description and boundaries. Website support prepares the addition, checks links and mobile presentation and shows a preview. Publication follows approval. If the service also appears in the Google Business Profile, a corresponding change is prepared there too.

## 4. Check function and impact proportionately

After a change, check the actual result first: Is the text accurate, are images clear and does the enquiry route work? Changes to page addresses should include planning suitable redirects and internal links.

Where suitable analytics access is available, search queries and website use can suggest further improvements. A search ranking alone does not explain enquiry quality or resulting revenue. Agree which data is reviewed and what conclusions it can actually support.

The [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) explains that changes take varying amounts of time and do not always have visible search effects. Support therefore cannot promise a fixed position or a particular number of new enquiries.

## 5. Start with a manageable task list

Gather the current gaps first: inaccurate details, missing services, broken links or awkward contact routes. Assign each task an owner and a next step. This provides a basis for a suitable support scope.
